There is a simple rule in homebuilding that almost nobody hears until it is too late: **the cost of a change goes up the later you make it.** A tweak in a 3D model is free. The same tweak on the framing crew's schedule is expensive, disruptive, and sometimes impossible. Building a full 3D model *before* the construction documents are finalized is the cheapest insurance you can buy on a custom home.

## You cannot approve what you cannot see

A floor plan is a set of lines that you, the homeowner, are expected to translate into three dimensions in your head. Most people cannot — and there is no shame in that. What they *can* do is look at a **photoreal model** of their actual home and immediately know: this ceiling feels low, that window is in the wrong place, this material is not what I imagined.

Catching those reactions in the model means catching them when they cost nothing to fix.

## The changes that hurt the most

The expensive surprises during construction are almost never small. They are structural or spatial:

- A room that feels cramped once the walls are up.
- A roofline that does not look how it was imagined.
- Windows that miss the view, or face the afternoon sun.
- Ceiling heights and volumes that read completely differently in real life.

Every one of these is trivial to explore and resolve in a model. Every one of these is painful to fix in the field.

## Documentation built on a decision, not a guess

When the 3D model is approved first, the construction documents are drawn to reflect a home you have already signed off on. The set is not a guess to be corrected later — it is the record of a decision already made. That makes the documents cleaner, the permit review smoother, and the build calmer.

## The order that saves money

1. **Design** the home to the lot and the zoning.
2. **Model** it in 3D and refine until you love it.
3. **Document** the approved home into a coordinated permit set.
4. **Build** from a set nobody has to second-guess.

Do it in that order and the 3D model pays for itself many times over — in changes you never have to make once the concrete is poured.
