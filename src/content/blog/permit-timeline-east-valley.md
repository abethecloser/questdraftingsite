It is the first question almost every homeowner asks, and it is the hardest to answer with a single number: **how long until I have a permit?** The truthful answer is that it depends on the project, the jurisdiction, and — more than anything — how clean your submittal is. But that does not mean the timeline is a mystery. Here is what actually moves the needle.

## The three phases before the city ever sees it

Long before a permit clock starts, three things have to happen:

1. **Design** — resolving the home itself: the plan, the elevations, how it sits on the lot.
2. **3D and approval** — confirming you love the actual home before it gets documented.
3. **Construction documents** — producing the coordinated, code-current set.

Rushing these to "get to the city faster" almost always backfires, because an incomplete set comes straight back as corrections.

## Then comes plan review

Once submitted, your jurisdiction reviews the set against its codes and checklists. They may approve it, or — more commonly on a first submittal — return **plan-review comments**: questions and required corrections. How long this takes depends on the city's current workload and the complexity of your project.

## What actually determines your timeline

In our experience, the single biggest factor is **how few correction cycles you go through.** A submittal that is coordinated, complete, and formatted to the jurisdiction's checklist can clear in far fewer rounds than one that is missing sheets or contradicts itself. Every avoided correction cycle is weeks saved.

That is why we format submittals to match each jurisdiction, answer redlines quickly, and manage resubmittals — the goal is to compress the number of trips through review, not just to submit fast.

## How to keep things moving

- Get the design and 3D **right** before documentation.
- Submit a **complete, coordinated** set the first time.
- Respond to comments **promptly** — reviewers move faster when you do.
- Keep **one point of contact** accountable for the whole process.

A permit is not something that happens to your project at the end. It is something you set up for success at the very beginning.
